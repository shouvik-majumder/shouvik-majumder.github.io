Cell-type-specific plasticity and neocortical dynamics during motor learning

Neocortical spiking dynamics underlie voluntary behavior and are thought to emerge through synaptic plasticity during learning, but the causal role of plasticity in different cortical cell types in shaping population dynamics has been unclear. In my doctoral work with Hidehiko Inagaki, we manipulated Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), a key mediator of synaptic plasticity, in mice learning a motor timing task. Transient CaMKII inactivation in the premotor cortex (anterior lateral motor cortex, ALM) impaired learning without affecting execution of already learned actions.

Using cell-type-specific manipulations, we found that CaMKII-dependent plasticity in two pyramidal tract (PT) neuron subtypes, but not in intratelencephalic (IT) neurons, was required for learning. With concurrent large-scale electrophysiology, we found that CaMKII activity in the two PT subtypes was necessary to shape distinct aspects of premotor cortical dynamics that jointly anticipate motor timing, whereas IT neuron plasticity was required to reduce the dimensionality of cortical activity. We conclude that synaptic plasticity in the major cortical cell types plays specialized and complementary roles in sculpting cortical dynamics during learning.

Majumder et al., Nature Communications 17, 8353 (2026). doi:10.1038/s41467-026-74869-1

Dopamine sensor development

In collaboration with the Tian lab, I contributed to the in vivo characterization of dLight3.8, a fluorescence-intensity and lifetime-based dopamine sensor with an expanded dynamic range. Using the sensor during motor learning, we observed a region-specific, gradual shift in dopamine encoding from tracking lick timing to signaling reward prediction.

Roshgadol*, Chouinard*, Majumder†, Scott†, et al. In revision. Preprint

Topological data analysis of EEG

In my master's thesis at IISER Kolkata, with Koel Das, I used topological data analysis to study single-trial EEG from children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and typically developing children while they viewed images of emotional faces and non-face objects. Using features derived from persistent homology, I classified ASD from typically developing participants with approximately 90% accuracy. Classification was possible as early as 120 ms after stimulus onset and did not depend on whether the image was a face, indicating that topological features of early visual and emotional processing in EEG are a candidate biomarker for ASD.

Majumder, Apicella, Muratori, Das. IEEE ICASSP (2020). doi:10.1109/ICASSP40776.2020.9054747
