I'm a PhD candidate in neuroscience at the Max Planck Florida Institute for Neuroscience and Florida Atlantic University, where I work with Hidehiko Inagaki as part of the IMPRS for Circuits and Synapses. My research asks how cell-type-specific synaptic plasticity reshapes the dynamics of neocortical circuits as animals learn — bringing together in vivo Neuropixels recordings, optogenetics, and dynamical-systems modeling.

I came to neuroscience from mathematics and physics. I trained at IISER Kolkata (BS–MS, mathematics major with minors in physics and biology), and I'm still drawn to problems where geometry, topology, and dynamical systems meet neural computation. Broadly, I'm interested in computational and systems neuroscience, NeuroAI, and the mathematical structure of learning in biological and artificial systems.

Research interests

  • Cortical dynamics of learning. How cell-type-specific plasticity sculpts premotor cortical activity as animals learn to time actions.
  • Dynamical-systems models of cortex. Low-dimensional descriptions of population activity and how plasticity reshapes them.
  • Geometry & topology of neural data. Topological data analysis and geometric methods for decoding neural signals.
  • NeuroAI. Connections between learning in biological circuits and in artificial systems.
