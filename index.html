I am a PhD candidate in neuroscience at the Max Planck Florida Institute for Neuroscience and Florida Atlantic University, working with Hidehiko Inagaki as part of the IMPRS for Circuits and Synapses program. In my research, we study how synaptic plasticity in specific cell types shapes the dynamics of population spiking activity in neocortical circuits during learning, using in vivo large-scale extracellular electrophysiology, optogenetics, and dynamical-systems modeling.

I came to neuroscience with a background in mathematics. I trained at IISER Kolkata (BS–MS, mathematics major with minors in physics and biology). In my master's thesis, I applied topological data analysis to classification problems with EEG data from children with autism.

Broadly, I am interested in understanding how learning determines the geometry and dynamical properties of representations in biological and artificial intelligent systems.

Research interests

  • Cortical dynamics during learning. How cell-type-specific plasticity shapes premotor cortical activity as animals learn new behavior.
  • Dynamical-systems models of cortex. Low-dimensional descriptions of population activity and how they are reshaped by plasticity and learning.
  • Geometry and topology of neural representations. Topological and geometric methods to describe representations in biological and artificial neural networks.
  • Planning and goal-directed behavior. How neural circuits select and sequence actions toward a goal, and how this is implemented in cortical and subcortical dynamics.
  • Generalization of task structure. How neural systems learn the abstract structure of a task and reuse it in new contexts, and which representations and algorithms support this transfer.
  • NeuroAI. Learning in artificial networks as a model of, and a tool for studying, learning in biological circuits.
